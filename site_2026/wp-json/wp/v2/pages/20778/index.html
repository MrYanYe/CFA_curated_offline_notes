Empirical Duration vs. Analytical Duration | CFA Level I Fixed Income<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we’ll explore the difference between empirical and analytical duration.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Understanding Duration<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Duration measures the sensitivity of a bond’s price to changes in interest rates. So far, we’ve discussed analytical duration<\/b>, which is based on mathematical analysis, such as expected future cash flows and maturity. Another approach is empirical duration<\/b>, which uses historical relationships between benchmark yields and bond prices.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Empirical Duration for Corporate Bonds<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

This distinction is particularly relevant for corporate bonds, which have a credit spread above the benchmark yield. Using analytical duration to estimate the change in the price of a corporate bond assumes that the credit spread remains constant, which might not be accurate.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
