Understanding Risk & Return in Equity Securities | CFA Level I Equity Investments<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this chapter, we’ll dive into the risk and return characteristics of different types of equity securities. Buckle up, and let’s get started!<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Breaking Down Equity Returns<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

First things first, let’s look at the main sources of equity returns:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Capital gains<\/b>: the difference between the selling price and purchase price.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Dividends<\/b>: payments received during the investment period.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    The total return<\/b> is the sum of capital gains and all dividends received, divided by the purchase price.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    EXAMPLE:<\/b> A share bought at \u20ac100, sold at \u20ac120, with \u20ac12 in dividends during the period would result in a total return of 32%.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Companies in different stages of their life cycle exhibit different return patterns:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Growth stocks<\/b>: Early-stage companies often pay little or no dividends, as earnings are reinvested for growth. Investors expect higher capital gains.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Value stocks<\/b>: Mature companies tend to pay higher dividends, as they have fewer growth opportunities. Investors may not expect significant stock price growth.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
