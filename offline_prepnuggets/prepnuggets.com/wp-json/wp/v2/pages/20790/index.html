Understanding Capital Structure and Seniority Rankings<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

The credit quality of a bond varies not only across different issuers but also among bonds issued by the same issuer. This variation is due to different seniority rankings<\/b>, or priority of payment, of the bonds.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

A company’s capital structure<\/b> refers to the composition and distribution across operating units of its liabilities and equity, including bank debt, bonds, preferred stock, and common equity. Some companies have simple capital structures, while others have more complex ones with multiple subsidiaries or operating companies issuing bonds with different levels of seniority rankings.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Secured and Unsecured Debt<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

Debt can be broadly classified into secured<\/b> and unsecured<\/b> debt:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Secured debt<\/b> means the bondholder has a direct claim on certain assets and their associated cash flows.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Unsecured bondholders<\/b> have only a general claim on an issuer’s assets and cash flows.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
