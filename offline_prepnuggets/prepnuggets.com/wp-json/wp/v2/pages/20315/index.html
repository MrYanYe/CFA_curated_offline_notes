Fiscal Policy Tools: Spending and Taxes | CFA Level I Economics<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we will explore the fiscal policy tools that governments use to achieve their macroeconomic goals. These tools can be classified as spending tools and revenue tools.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Spending Tools<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Spending tools include:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Transfer payments<\/b>: These are payments like social security and unemployment benefits that provide a minimum level of income for low-income households. They can also be used to redistribute income from the rich to the poor. Note that transfer payments are not included in GDP calculations.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Current spending<\/b>: This refers to government spending on an ongoing basis, such as health, education, and defense. This type of spending can impact a country’s skill level and overall labor productivity.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Capital spending<\/b>: This includes government spending on infrastructure, such as schools, public transport, and hospitals. Capital spending is expected to boost future productivity.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Government spending can be justified on both economic and social grounds, including providing basic services, ensuring a minimum standard of living, building infrastructure, supporting growth and unemployment targets, and subsidizing innovative and high-risk new products.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Government Revenues: Direct and Indirect Taxes<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Government revenues can take the form of:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Direct taxes<\/b>: These are levied on income and wealth, including personal and corporate income taxes, national insurance taxes, capital gains taxes, property tax, and inheritance tax.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Indirect taxes<\/b>: These are levied on goods and services, including sales taxes, value-added taxes, and excise taxes like tobacco and alcohol tax. Excise taxes can be used to reduce consumption of some goods and services like alcohol, tobacco, and gambling.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

      Taxes can be justified in terms of raising revenues to finance expenditures and for income and wealth redistribution. Economists consider four desirable attributes of tax policy: simplicity, efficiency, fairness, and sufficiency.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      Advantages and Disadvantages of Fiscal Policy Tools<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

      Using fiscal policy tools has its advantages and disadvantages:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

        \n
      • Advantages include the quick implementation of indirect taxes and the ability to implement social policies quickly through excise taxes.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • Disadvantages include the time it takes to implement direct taxes and transfer payments, as well as the long implementation time for capital spending projects.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

        Announcing changes in fiscal policy can also have significant effects on expectations, such as an announcement of future increases in personal income tax reducing current consumption.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

        Effectiveness of Fiscal Policy Tools on Aggregate Demand<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

        Not all fiscal policy tools affect aggregate demand equally. Spending tools are more effective in increasing aggregate demand, while tax reductions are somewhat less effective due to the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) effect.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
