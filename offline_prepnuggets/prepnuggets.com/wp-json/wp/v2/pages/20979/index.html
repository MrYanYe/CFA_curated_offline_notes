Mastering Order Instructions | CFA Level I Equity Investments<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we’ll explore the various types of instructions a trader can specify when placing a market order. These instructions can be classified as execution, validity, and clearing instructions. You might be familiar with some of these terms if you’ve traded stocks before.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Execution Instructions<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Execution instructions specify how to trade. The most common types are market orders<\/b> and limit orders<\/b>.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Market Order:<\/b> Instructs the broker to execute the trade immediately at the best possible price.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Limit Order:<\/b> Places a minimum execution price on sell orders and a maximum execution price on buy orders.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
    \n
    \n

    EXAMPLE<\/b><\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Margaret issues a market order to buy 4 shares, while Lee issues a limit order to buy 4 shares with a limit price of $233. If the sell queue when the orders were made are shown below, Margaret will have all 4 shares filled immediately, based on the best prices that in the queue.  For Lee\u2019s order, only the best ask is below the limit price of $233.  Therefore only 1 of the 4 slots will be filled at this instance.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
