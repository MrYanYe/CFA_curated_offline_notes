Non-Current Assets | CFA Level I FSA<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

Still on the topic of understanding balance sheets, let’s move on to the next major component – non-current assets<\/b>.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Overview of Non-Current Assets<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Assets, other than current assets, are collectively known as non-current or long-lived assets. Non-current assets can be sub-divided into 3 main categories:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Tangible assets<\/b> – physical assets such as plant, property, equipment, and investment property<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Intangible assets<\/b> – non-physical items like patents, trademarks, and goodwill<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Financial assets<\/b><\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Plant, Property, and Equipment (PP&E)<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    PP&E are tangible assets used in the production of goods and services. It includes land and buildings, machinery and equipment, furniture, and natural resources. PP&E can be reported in the balance sheet using the cost model or the revaluation model.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
