Financial Intermediaries: An In-Depth Overview | CFA Level I Equity Investments<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we’ll dive deeper into the different types of financial intermediaries and the essential services they provide in the world of finance.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

What are Financial Intermediaries?<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Financial intermediaries play a crucial role in any market, standing between buyers and sellers to facilitate the exchange of assets, capital, and risk. These services allow for greater efficiency and are vital to a well-functioning economy. Some key financial intermediaries include:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Brokers and exchanges<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Dealers<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Securitizers<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Depository institutions<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Insurance companies<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Arbitrageurs<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Clearinghouses<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Brokers and Exchanges: Facilitating Trades<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Brokers help clients buy and sell securities efficiently by finding suitable counterparties for trades. They may work for large brokerage firms, banks, or at exchanges. Block brokers specialize in placing large trades that are difficult to execute without moving the market.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Exchanges provide a venue where different parties can trade with each other, sometimes even acting as brokers themselves by providing electronic order matching. They also regulate their members and require listed firms to provide faithful and timely financial disclosures.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Alternative trading systems (ATS) serve the same trading function as exchanges but have no regulatory function. They are also known as electronic communication networks or multilateral trading facilities. Many ATS are known as dark pools, as they do not display the orders that their clients send to them.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
