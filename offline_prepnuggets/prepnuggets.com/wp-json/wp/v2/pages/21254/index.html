What is the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)?<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

The Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)<\/b> is the firm’s discount rate used to discount future cash flows for calculating the Net Present Value (NPV) of a project. To determine a meaningful rate, we need to understand the different components of a firm’s capital, which usually consist of debt, equity, and sometimes preferred stock.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Each component represents the required return demanded by investors to compensate them for the risk they take in supplying capital to the company. Typically, the cost of debt is lower than the cost of preferred stock, which is lower than the cost of common equity.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
