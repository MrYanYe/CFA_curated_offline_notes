Understanding Profitability Ratios and DuPont Analysis | CFA Level I FSA<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we’re going to explore profitability ratios and the DuPont analysis in greater detail.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Profitability Ratios: The Basics<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Profitability ratios measure a firm’s overall performance relative to its revenues, assets, equity, and capital. These ratios are essential for determining a firm’s value and understanding its financial health. Here are some key profitability ratios you need to know:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Gross profit margin<\/b>: Indicates the percentage of revenue available to cover expenses and generate profit after deducting product costs.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Operating profit margin<\/b>: Measures profitability after accounting for operating costs, like wages and rent.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Pretax margin<\/b>: Evaluates a firm’s profitability before accounting for income taxes, providing insight into management’s performance in driving profitability.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Net profit margin<\/b>: Includes both recurring and non-recurring components, giving a more comprehensive view of a company’s potential future profitability.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Return on assets (ROA)<\/b>: Measures the return earned by a firm on its assets, often calculated as net income divided by average total assets.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Return on equity (ROE)<\/b>: Measures the return earned by a company on its equity capital, including minority equity, preferred equity, and common equity.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Return on common equity<\/b>: Measures the return earned by a company only on its common equity, excluding preferred shareholder dividends from the numerator.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Return on total capital<\/b>: Measures the profits a company earns on all capital it employs, including short-term debt, long-term debt, and all equity.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
