Capital Market Theory | CFA Level I Portfolio Management<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

Welcome back as we continue our discussion on portfolio risk and return. Today, we’ll dive into capital market theory and explore the special case of the capital allocation line known as the capital market line.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Quick Recap of Key Concepts<\/h2>\n\n\n\n
    \n
  • Efficient frontier<\/b> of risky assets is formed from the expected risk and returns of all investable risky assets.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Capital allocation line (CAL)<\/b> is optimal when tangential to the efficient frontier.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Optimal risky portfolio<\/b> sits at the intersection of the CAL and the efficient frontier.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Investor’s optimal portfolio<\/b> is found when combined with their indifference curve.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Expected return<\/b> of the portfolio is the weighted average of the risk-free rate and the risky rate of return.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Standard deviation of returns<\/b> is the weight of the risky portfolio multiplied by its standard deviation.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Capital Market Theory and the Capital Market Line<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Under the simplifying assumption that investors have homogeneous expectations<\/b>, all investors face the same efficient frontier and have the same optimal capital allocation line, termed the capital market line (CML)<\/b>.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
