Understanding Residential Mortgage-Backed Securities | CFA Level I Fixed Income<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

Residential Mortgage-Backed Securities (RMBS)<\/b> are a popular type of asset-backed security. In the US, RMBS are divided into three sectors:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Securities guaranteed by a federal agency, such as Ginnie Mae<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Securities guaranteed by government-sponsored enterprises (GSEs), like Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Non-agency RMBS issued by private entities<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Agency vs. Non-Agency RMBS<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Agency RMBS are backed by a federal agency or GSE, while non-agency RMBS are issued by private companies. The main differences between the two include:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Credit risk reduction:<\/b> Agency RMBS have reduced credit risk due to GSE guarantees, while non-agency RMBS use credit enhancements.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Underwriting standards:<\/b> Loans in agency RMBS must meet specific criteria, while non-agency RMBS can include non-conforming mortgages.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
