Equity & Credit Analysis: Key Concepts and Techniques | CFA Level I FSA<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this article, we’ll delve into the world of equity and credit analysis. We’ll explore valuation ratios, segment reporting, and forecasting models to help you better understand the intricacies of financial analysis.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Valuing Equities: Ratios and Quantities<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Equity analysis evaluates a company’s performance and values its equity to assess its investment attractiveness. Analysts use various methods, including valuation ratios and per-share quantities, to achieve this goal.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Valuation Ratios<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

Some popular valuation ratios are:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Price-to-Earnings (P\/E) ratio:<\/b> Price per share divided by earnings per share (EPS). It indicates how much an investor pays for each dollar of earnings.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Price-to-Cash Flow ratio:<\/b> An alternative to P\/E ratio, as net income can be more susceptible to manipulation.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Price-to-Sales ratio:<\/b> Useful when earnings or cash flow is negative; calculated by dividing price per share by sales per share.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Price-to-Book (P\/B) ratio:<\/b> Indicates market judgment about the relationship between a company’s required rate of return and its actual rate of return.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Per-Share Quantities<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

    Per-share quantities include earnings per share (EPS), which can be expressed as Basic EPS<\/b> and Diluted EPS<\/b>. Other per-share measures include cash flow per share, EBITDA per share, and dividends per share.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Dividend-Related Quantities<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Dividend-related quantities help in valuing equities:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Dividend Payout Ratio:<\/b> Measures the percentage of earnings paid out as dividends to shareholders.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Retention Rate:<\/b> Complement of the dividend payout ratio; represents the proportion of earnings retained for company growth.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Sustainable Growth Rate:<\/b> A function of profitability (ROE) and retention rate; indicates how fast a firm can grow without additional external capital.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

      Industry-Specific Ratios<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

      Different industries have unique ratios that are considered important for analysis. For example:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

        \n
      • Retail industry: Growth in same-store sales, sales per square foot.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • Service companies: Revenue per employee, net income per employee.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • Regulated industries: Capital adequacy, cash reserve ratio, liquid asset ratio, net interest margin.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

        Credit Analysis Ratios<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

        Credit analysis assesses a company’s ability to service and repay its debt. Some key ratios used in credit analysis are:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

          \n
        • Interest Coverage Ratios<\/li>\n\n\n\n
        • Return on Capital<\/li>\n\n\n\n
        • Debt-to-Assets Ratio<\/li>\n\n\n\n
        • Cash Flows to Total Debt Ratios<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

          Segment Reporting<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

          Segment reporting helps analysts evaluate conglomerates by breaking down financial data into individual business or geographic segments. Useful ratios that can be computed from segment data include segment margin, segment return on assets (ROA), segment turnover, and segment debt ratio.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

          Financial Analysis Techniques: Integration and Forecasting<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

          Analysts integrate the results of ratio and common-size analysis with other data to build models for forecasting future performance. Such data includes analyst assumptions and judgments, as well as economic and industry data.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
