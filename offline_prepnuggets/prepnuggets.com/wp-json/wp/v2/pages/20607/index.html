A Quick Recap of Bonds I<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Before diving in, let’s quickly revise what we learned in the previous lesson:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • When a bond is issued at a discount, cash received is recorded on the balance sheet with a corresponding bond liability.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • The liability increases over time to the face value of $100,000 at maturity.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Interest expense on the income statement equals the book value multiplied by the effective rate.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • In the statement of cash flow, the issue proceeds are reported as a cash inflow from financing activities.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Under US GAAP, coupon payments are reported as CFO, while under IFRS, they may be reported as CFO or CFF.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • At maturity, the repayment of the face value is reported as a cash outflow from financing, and the cash and bond liability are removed from the balance sheet.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Issuance Costs and Accounting Issues<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Issuing a bond involves legal and accounting fees, printing costs, sales commissions, and other fees. Let’s see how these issuance costs are handled under U.S. GAAP and IFRS:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    U.S. GAAP:<\/b><\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Issuance costs are capitalized as a deferred charge.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • This capitalized asset is allocated to the income statement as an expense over the term of the bond.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • On the cash flow statement, issuance costs are netted against bond proceeds and reported as a financing cash flow.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

      IFRS:<\/b><\/p>\n\n\n\n

        \n
      • Initial bond liability on the balance sheet is reduced by the amount of issuance costs.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • This increases the bond’s effective interest rate.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • First year interest expense is calculated based on the increased effective rate.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • Issuance costs are treated as unamortized discount under IFRS.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • Like U.S. GAAP, bond issuance costs are netted against bond proceeds and reported as a financing cash flow.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
