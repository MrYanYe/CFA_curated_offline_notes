Understanding Sovereign Debt | CFA Level I<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

Today, we’re diving into the fascinating world of government and government-related debt. This introductory lesson will cover the characteristics, issuance, and trading of sovereign debt. <\/p>\n\n\n\n

What is Sovereign Debt?<\/b><\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Sovereign debt, in simple terms, is money borrowed by national governments to fund their activities, including the maintenance of public goods and services. Governments have the authority to levy taxes within their jurisdiction, serving as a primary cash flow source. Other revenue streams include tariffs, usage fees, and profits from state-owned enterprises.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Government Financials: A Closer Look<\/b><\/h2>\n\n\n\n

The financial balance sheet of a government encompasses a variety of assets and liabilities. Assets might include short-term claims, real estate, infrastructure, and government enterprises, along with reserves in foreign currency and commodities like gold. Liabilities cover domestic and foreign debt, both short and long-term, along with obligations to external entities and state pensions.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
