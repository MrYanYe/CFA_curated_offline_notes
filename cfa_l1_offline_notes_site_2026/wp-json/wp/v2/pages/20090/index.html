Pricing Strategies Across Market Structures | CFA Level I Economics<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

n this lesson, we’ll summarize the pricing strategies of the four types of market structures we’ve learned about: perfectly competitive markets, monopolistically competitive markets, oligopolies, and monopolies. By comparing these market structures side-by-side, you’ll better comprehend and remember their differences.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Golden Rule for Profit Maximization<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Before we discuss the pricing strategies for each market structure, remember this golden rule<\/b>: a firm’s profits are maximized when it produces at the point where the firm’s marginal revenue equals marginal cost (MR = MC<\/strong>).<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Pricing Strategies for Different Market Structures<\/h2>\n\n\n\n
    \n
  1. Perfect Competition:<\/b> Firms in perfectly competitive markets are price takers, meaning their demand curve is horizontal. In this case, the market price is the marginal revenue and also the average revenue. Firms will produce at the profit-maximizing output where MR = MC.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  2. Monopolistic Competition:<\/b> In this market structure, firms differentiate their products, resulting in a downward-sloping demand curve for each firm. The marginal revenue curve is also downward-sloping, but with a steeper gradient. Firms will produce at the profit-maximizing output where MR = MC, earning economic profit in the short run. However, in the long run, new entrants attracted by economic profit will increase competition, lowering the firm’s demand curve, reducing output, and eliminating economic profit.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  3. Monopoly:<\/b> A monopolist’s demand curve is the same as the market’s demand curve, which is downward-sloping. Monopolists also produce at the profit-maximizing output where MR = MC. Due to high entry barriers, monopolists can maintain economic profit in the long run.<\/li>\n<\/ol>\n\n\n\n
