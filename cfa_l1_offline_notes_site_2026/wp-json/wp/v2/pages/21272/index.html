Capital Structure and Company Life Cycle | CFA Level I Corporate Issuers<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we’re exploring capital structure and how it evolves throughout a company’s life cycle. <\/p>\n\n\n\n

Understanding Capital Structure<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Capital structure refers to the way a company finances its assets and operations using a mix of equity and debt issuance. When discussing a company’s debt-to-equity ratios in the context of capital structure, it’s important to remember that we use market values<\/b> for both debt and equity, not book values from the balance sheet. For example, if the market value of a company’s debt is $2M and the market value of its outstanding shares is $3M, we describe the capital structure as 40% debt, 60% equity, and its debt-to-equity ratio is 67%.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Capital Structure and the Company Life Cycle<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Companies adopt a wide range of capital structures based on their life cycle stage. The main characteristics that influence the proportion of debt in a company’s capital structure are:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • The growth and stability of revenue.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • The growth and predictability of cash flow.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • The amount of business risk.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • The availability of debt<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • The cost of debt financing.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • The amount and liquidity of the company’s assets.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
