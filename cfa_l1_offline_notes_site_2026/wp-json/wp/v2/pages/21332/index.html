Exploring Different Types of Investors | CFA Level I Portfolio Management<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we’ll introduce different types of investors and highlight their unique characteristics and needs. We’ll focus on four main characteristics: investment horizon, risk tolerance, income needs, and liquidity needs.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Individual Investors: From Youngsters to Retirees<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Individual investors save and invest for various reasons, such as buying a home, funding children’s education, or planning for retirement. Depending on their life stage, their needs will differ:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Young individuals<\/b>: These investors usually have a long investment horizon and high risk tolerance, with low income and liquidity needs. They tend to seek assets with strong capital gains potential, like growth stocks and commodities.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Retirees<\/b>: With a shorter investment horizon and higher income and liquidity needs, retirees have a lower risk tolerance. They may invest in fixed-income and dividend-paying stocks.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Institutional Investors: A Diverse Landscape<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    There are various types of institutional investors, including defined benefit pension plans, endowments, foundations, banks, insurance companies, investment companies, and sovereign wealth funds. Let’s take a closer look at each.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Defined Benefit Pension Plans<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

    In defined benefit pension plans, employers commit to paying a fixed annual amount to employees upon retirement. The employer assumes the investment risk and must contribute enough to ensure the fund can provide promised future benefits.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
