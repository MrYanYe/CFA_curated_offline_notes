Decoding Inventory Management and Ratios | CFA Level I<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

As we conclude our exploration of inventories, we turn our focus to the crucial aspect of presentation disclosures related to inventory and delve into the analytical power of inventory ratios. This session promises to enhance your understanding of how inventories are reported and analyzed within the realms of IFRS and US GAAP. Let’s embark on this informative journey.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Understanding Presentation Disclosures<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

When it comes to inventory reporting, the carrying value at the beginning and end of the period, along with reconciliations, are essential. Both IFRS and US GAAP mandate similar disclosures, including:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Accounting Policies<\/b>: Specification of the cost flow method employed, be it LIFO, FIFO, or another method.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Total Carrying Value<\/b>: Disclosure of the ending inventory’s total value, with a breakdown for specific industries, such as raw materials, work-in-progress, and finished goods for manufacturing firms.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Cost of Sales<\/b>: The amount of inventory recognized as sold during the period, essentially the cost of goods sold on the income statement.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Reversals and Write-downs<\/b>: Disclosures regarding any reversals of past write-downs (IFRS only) and any write-downs recognized as an expense during the period.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Collateral Pledges<\/b>: Information on inventories pledged as collateral, if applicable.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
