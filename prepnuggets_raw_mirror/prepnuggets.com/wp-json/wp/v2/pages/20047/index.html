Factors of Production<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Imagine you are running a cafe. The product of your cafe is serving coffee, and obviously, you need several inputs to make that happen. In economics terms, we call these inputs the factors of production, which can include:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Land<\/b> – which, in this case, is the physical space you own or rent to house your cafe.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Labour<\/b> – this includes all workers that you hire, from unskilled to top management.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Physical capital<\/b> – sometimes called plant and equipment. In your case, this includes the coffee machine, tables, chairs, and kitchen equipment. Note that this does not include financial capital.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Materials<\/b> – which refers to the inputs that make the production possible. This should include the coffee beans, milk, water, and electricity.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    For simplicity, economists typically concentrate on just two of these, labor and physical capital, denoted by letters L and K.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
