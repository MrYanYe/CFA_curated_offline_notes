Multiplier Models: Price Multiple Model | CFA Level I Equity Investments<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we will explore the price multiple model, which includes two approaches: price multiples based on fundamentals and price multiples based on comparables.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Price Multiples and Common Ratios<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Price multiples are calculated by dividing the stock price by a firm fundamental. Common price multiples include:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Price-to-earnings ratio (P\/E)<\/b> – the company’s stock price divided by its earnings per share.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Price-to-sales ratio (P\/S)<\/b> – the stock price divided by sales per share.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Price-to-book value ratio (P\/B)<\/b> – the stock price divided by book value of equity per share.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Price-to-cash flow ratio (P\/CF)<\/b> – the stock price divided by cash flow per share. Cash flow can be defined as operating cash flow or free cash flow.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Price multiples are widely used due to their easy calculation and use in time series and cross-sectional comparisons. However, a common criticism is that price multiples often reflect the past since trailing data is used in the denominator. To address this, some analysts use forward values based on the company’s projected finances for the following year.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    There are 2 approaches to using price multiples for valuation – based on comparables, and based on fundamentals.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
