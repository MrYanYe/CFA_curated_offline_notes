Economies and Diseconomies of Scale | CFA Level I Economics<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this quick lesson, we’ll proceed to economies and diseconomies of scale and how they affect your business decisions in the long run.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Understanding Economies of Scale<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

In the short run, your cafe’s average total cost may be constrained due to factors like limited space and equipment. However, as you open more cafes in the long run, the average costs can decrease as production increases. This effect is called economies of scale<\/b>.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Economies of scale occur when the cost per unit of production falls as the firm increases its scale. They generally result from factors such as:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Labor specialization<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Mass production<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Investment in more efficient equipment and technology<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Increased bargaining power with suppliers<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    As long as there are economies of scale, expanding your business can improve its competitiveness and profitability.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
