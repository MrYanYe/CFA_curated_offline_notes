Non-current liabilities can include long-term debt, such as bonds and long-term notes, finance leases, pension liabilities, and deferred tax liabilities. This topic focuses on bonds and leases, as well as an introduction on pension liabilities.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

In this first part, we shall learn about bond issuance.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Understanding Bonds<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

A bond is a contract between a lender, and a borrower. The lender is also known as the bondholder, while the borrower is also known as the bond issuer.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

There are 4 important components stated on the bond:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Maturity date<\/b>: This is the date that the principle will be returned to the bondholder, and the contract completed.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Face value or par value<\/b>: This is the amount of principle that will be paid to the bondholder on the maturity date.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Coupon rate<\/b>: This is the stated interest that is used to calculate the periodic interest payment to the bondholder.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Payout frequency<\/b>: Most coupon bearing bonds pay out coupons semi-annually.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Example of Bond Issuance<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Let us assume that this is a new bond issue that will mature in 3 years time, with a face value of $100,000, a coupon rate of 6%, which is to be paid out annually.<\/b><\/p>\n\n\n\n

    At issuance, there are 3 scenarios that can happen. The bond can be issued at par value, at a discount, or at a premium. Let\u2019s examine the implications of each of these scenarios.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Bonds Issued at Par<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

    When a bond is issued at par, the lender pays the bond issuer the exact face value of the bond, which in this case is $100,000. In return, the lender is promised regular coupon payments, and the return of the par value at maturity. The coupon payment is calculated by multiplying the coupon rate with the face value.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    So in this case, the lender pays $100,000 to the bond issuer to buy the bond. For the next 3 years, the bond issuer is obligated to make annual coupon payments of $6,000 each time. The face value of $100,000 will be repaid to the bondholder on the maturity date.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    A frequently quoted attribute of a bond is the effective interest rate of the bond.<\/b> Not to be confused with the coupon rate, the effective rate is the interest rate that equates the present value of the future cash flows, with the issue price. If you punch in these figures into your calculator, you will derive an effective interest rate of 6%.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
