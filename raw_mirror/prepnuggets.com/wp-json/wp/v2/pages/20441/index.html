Geopolitics: Tools, Risks, and Investments | CFA Level I Economics<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we’ll dive deeper into geopolitics, focusing on the types of geopolitical tools and how to analyze geopolitical risks.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Types of Geopolitical Tools<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Geopolitical tools are the means by which state actors advance their interests in the world. They can be classified as:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • National security tools<\/b><\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Economic tools<\/b><\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Financial tools<\/b><\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    These tools can be used to facilitate cooperation or escalate conflict, and they may advance globalization or nationalism.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    National Security Tools<\/h3>\n\n\n\n
      \n
    • Armed conflict, espionage, and terrorism are examples of national security tools.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • These tools are generally non-cooperative and advance nationalism.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Military alliances, like NATO, can advance cooperation but may also create divisions between members and non-members.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

      Economic Tools<\/h3>\n\n\n\n
        \n
      • Examples include multilateral trade agreements (e.g., WTO) and common markets (e.g., European Union).<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • Non-cooperative economic tools include nationalization, tariffs, quotas, and voluntary export restraints.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
      • Domestic content requirements can also be considered nationalistic economic tools.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

        Financial Tools<\/h3>\n\n\n\n
          \n
        • Cooperative financial tools include free exchange of currencies and allowing foreign investment.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
        • Restrictions on currency exchange and foreign investment are non-cooperative financial tools.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
        • Sanctions can also be used as financial tools to create conflicts.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
