Long-Term Financial Forecasting Essentials | CFA Level I<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

Today, we’re venturing into the domain of long-term financial forecasting, moving beyond the short-term horizon of 1 to 2 years to consider forecasts spanning several years into the future. This lesson will guide you through determining an appropriate forecast horizon, tackling the complexities of long-term forecasts, and understanding the concept of terminal value. <\/p>\n\n\n\n

Choosing the Right Forecast Horizon<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

The forecast horizon is pivotal and should align with the investment strategy or specific requirements, such as:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Individual investment horizons, e.g., planning to hold a stock for 6 years.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • The cyclical nature of the business, ensuring the forecast covers at least one business cycle.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Recent significant events like acquisitions or restructuring, allowing time for their effects to materialize.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Considerations for Long-Term Forecasts<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Long-term forecasts focus on a company’s normalized earnings potential, taking into account:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Business cycles and their impact on mid-cycle sales and profits.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Recent M&A activities, restructuring, and other one-off events.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Revenue projection as the foundation, with subsequent income statement items derived from it.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
