Understanding Current Liabilities<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Current liabilities are obligations that will be satisfied within one year or one operating cycle, whichever is greater.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Accounts payable<\/b>, or trade payables, are amounts the firm owes to suppliers for goods or services purchased on credit. When the firm receives the goods before making payment, the amount owed is recorded as an accounts payable under liabilities.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Notes payable<\/b> are short-term obligations in the form of promissory notes owed to creditors and lenders.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Accrued liabilities<\/b> are expenses that have been recognized in the income statement but have not yet been paid as of the balance sheet date.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Unearned revenue<\/b> is cash collected in advance of providing goods and services.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Accounts Payable<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

    When the amount owed is paid, cash is reduced, and the accounts payable is reduced by the corresponding amount. Analyzing payables relative to purchases can signal credit problems with suppliers.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
