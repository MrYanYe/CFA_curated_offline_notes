Mastering Revenue Recognition | CFA Level I FSA<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this study note, we’ll cover revenue recognition, a crucial topic for understanding financial statements. We’ll dive into the five-step process for recognizing revenue and explore some examples to help solidify your understanding.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Revenue Recognition and Accrual Accounting<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Under the accrual method<\/b> of accounting, revenue is recognized when earned, regardless of cash payment. To illustrate:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • When a firm provides goods or services before receiving cash payment, the revenue is recorded in the income statement, and accounts receivable in the balance sheet increases.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • When a customer pays cash first before receiving goods or services, cash is recorded, and unearned revenue is recorded as a liability in the balance sheet. Revenue is not recognized at this point.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • When the goods or service is fulfilled, the revenue is recognized on the income statement, and the liability is reduced.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    It’s essential to have accounting standards in place to prevent companies from manipulating net income using accrual methods.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    IASB and FASB’s Converged Standards for Revenue Recognition<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    The IASB and FASB issued converged standards for revenue recognition in 2018. The new standards use a principles-based approach with a central principle: a firm should recognize revenue when it has transferred a good or service to a customer.
