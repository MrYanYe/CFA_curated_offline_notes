    Definition and Conventions of Foreign Exchange Rates<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    An exchange rate<\/b> is simply the price of one currency in terms of another. The CFA curriculum uses this convention:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Price currency:<\/b> The currency on top<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Base currency:<\/b> The currency below<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

      The exchange rate is the price of one unit of the base currency in terms of the price currency.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      For example, if the exchange rate of USD\/GBP is 1.3, it means that the price of 1 GBP is USD $1.30.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      Keep in mind that there are alternative conventions for foreign exchange quotes, so don’t be surprised to see the same quote expressed in the opposite way. The CFA Institute’s convention is more intuitive.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      Direct and Indirect Quotes<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

      Direct quote:<\/b> From the point of view of an investor in the price currency country<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      Indirect quote:<\/b> From the point of view of an investor in the base currency country<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      For example, a USD\/GBP quote of 1.3 is a direct exchange rate quote for a USD-based investor and an indirect quote for a GBP-based investor.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
