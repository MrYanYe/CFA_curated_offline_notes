Understanding Activity, Liquidity, and Solvency Ratios | CFA Level I FSA<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this article, we’ll dive into three types of financial ratios that analysts frequently use: activity ratios, liquidity ratios, and solvency ratios. We’ll also explain how to compute, analyze, and interpret these ratios.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Types of Financial Ratios<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

In financial analysis, we can broadly classify ratios into five categories:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Activity Ratios:<\/b> Measure the efficiency of day-to-day tasks, like collecting receivables and managing inventory.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Liquidity Ratios:<\/b> Measure the company’s ability to meet short-term obligations.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Solvency Ratios:<\/b> Measure the company’s ability to meet long-term obligations. Also known as leverage and long-term debt ratios.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Profitability Ratios:<\/b> Measure the company’s ability to generate profits from its assets.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Valuation Ratios:<\/b> Measure the quantity of an asset or flow associated with ownership of a specified claim.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    These categories aren’t mutually exclusive, but each ratio measures a different aspect of the company’s business. In this article, we’ll focus on activity, liquidity, and solvency ratios.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
