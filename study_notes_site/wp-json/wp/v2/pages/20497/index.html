Now that we have a deeper understanding of the income statement, we move on to the topic of understanding balance sheets. In this first part, we start with a basic understanding of the balance sheet – its main components and the presentation formats.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Elements of a Balance Sheet<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

The balance sheet can be viewed as a snapshot<\/b> of a firm’s financial position. It consists of 3 elements:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Assets<\/b>, which are resources controlled by the firm. This includes cash that the firm is holding.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Liabilities<\/b>, which are amounts owed to creditors and lenders.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Owner’s Equity<\/b>, which is the owners’ residual claim on the company’s assets after deducting its liabilities.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    It is called the balance sheet<\/b> because the amount of assets must always be equal to the sum of liabilities and owner’s equity.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
