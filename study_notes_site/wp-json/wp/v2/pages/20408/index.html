Welcome back as we discuss capital restrictions and the reasons why some governments impose them.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

What are Capital Restrictions?<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Capital restrictions<\/b> are policies aimed at controlling the flow of financial capital across borders. Such controls are placed on foreigners’ ability to own domestic assets, or domestic residents’ ability to own foreign assets. Thus, in contrast with trade restrictions, which limit the openness of goods markets, capital restrictions limit the openness of financial markets.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Restrictions on capital inflow can include:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Outright prohibition of foreign investment in the domestic economy.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Requiring majority domestic ownership of certain firms.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Prohibition of foreign investment in certain domestic industries.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Restrictions on capital outflow include:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Taxes on the income earned on foreign investments by domestic citizens.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Restrictions on repatriation of earnings by foreign firms operating in the country.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

      Economic Impact of Capital Restrictions<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

      Economists consider free movement of financial capital to be beneficial because it allows capital to be invested where it will earn the highest return. So overall, capital restrictions are thought to decrease economic welfare in both the sources and recipients of capital.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      In spite of the negative impact of capital restrictions, some governments place capital restrictions for various reasons.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
