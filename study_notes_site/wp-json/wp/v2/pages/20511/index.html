Analysis of Balance Sheets | CFA Level I FSA<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

We’re diving into some techniques for analysing a balance sheet<\/b>. Analyzing a company’s balance sheet can provide insight into the company’s liquidity and solvency, as well as the economic resources the company controls. We’ll examine the balance sheets for 2 hypothetical companies, Giant and Pixie, to help us with our analysis.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Liquidity and Solvency Ratios<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

One commonly used tool is to convert balance sheet figures into ratios. There are two main classes of balance sheet ratios:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Liquidity Ratios<\/b>: These measure the firm’s ability to satisfy its short-term obligations.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Solvency Ratios<\/b>: These measure the firm’s ability to satisfy its long-term obligations.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Liquidity Ratios<\/h3>\n\n\n\n

    Liquidity ratios evaluate if the amount of liquid assets are sufficient to satisfy the short-term debt due within a year. Common liquidity ratios include:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    • Current Ratio<\/b>: Ratio of current assets to current liabilities.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Quick Ratio<\/b>: Ratio of cash plus marketable securities plus receivables, to current liabilities. Excludes inventory from the calculation.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    • Cash Ratio<\/b>: Cash plus marketable securities divided by current liabilities. Excludes both inventory and receivables from the calculation.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n
