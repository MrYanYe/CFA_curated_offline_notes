Corporate Governance Overview and Stakeholder Groups | CFA Level I Corporate Issuers<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this lesson, we’ll discuss the definition of corporate governance and the various stakeholder groups involved in the process.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

What is Corporate Governance?<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Corporate governance is the response to high-profile company failures caused by weak governance. Regulations have been introduced to protect financial markets and investors, who have also become more attentive to environmental and social issues related to a company’s operations. This brings us to ESG, which we’ll discuss later on.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

According to the CFA Institute:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Corporate governance is a framework for a listed company<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • It defines the rights, roles, and responsibilities of various groups<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Conflicting interests between groups exist<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • A system of checks, balances, and incentives should be arranged to minimize and manage the conflicts<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Shareholder Theory vs. Stakeholder Theory<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    There are two main theories for corporate governance:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

      \n
    1. Shareholder Theory:<\/b> Primarily focuses on the interests of the firm’s shareholders, which is to maximize the value of the firm’s common stock. Under this theory, corporate governance is primarily concerned with the conflict of interest between the firm’s managers and the shareholders.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
    2. Stakeholder Theory:<\/b> Considers the conflicts among the several groups that have an interest in the activities and performance of the company, including shareholders, board of directors, management, employees, suppliers, creditors, and others.<\/li>\n<\/ol>\n\n\n\n
