Credit Cycles Explained | CFA Level I Economics<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

In this short lesson, we’ll quickly dive into the concept of credit cycles and how they relate to business cycles.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Understanding Credit Cycles<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

We’ve learned about business cycles<\/b> in a previous lesson, which uses GDP as a measure of economic activity. On the other hand, credit cycles<\/b> refer to cyclical fluctuations in interest rates and the availability of loans. The bottom of the cycle is characterized by high interest rates and low availability of loans, while the peak of the cycle features low interest rates and wide availability of loans. The two cycles are intertwined:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Lenders are more willing to lend and tend to offer lower interest rates during economic expansions.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Lenders are less willing to lend and require higher interest rates when the economy is contracting.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    The effect goes the other way as well. When credit is cheap and widely available, asset bubbles can form, particularly in the equity and property market. When credit subsequently tightens and gets more expensive, asset prices plunge and lead to a domino effect of defaults. Research suggests that when the directions of the business cycle coincide with the directions of the credit cycles, economic expansions tend to be stronger, and contractions are deeper and longer.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
