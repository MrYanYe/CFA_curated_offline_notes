Detailed Financial Forecasting Techniques | CFA Level I<\/h1>\n\n\n\n

Embark on an advanced journey through financial forecasting, focusing on income statement and balance sheet items crucial for assessing a company’s future. This lesson covers revenue, cost of sales, SG&A, and working capital forecasting in depth.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Revenue Forecasting: Drivers and Approaches<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Utilize both top-down and bottom-up approaches<\/b> to identify key revenue drivers. For instance, projecting a gym chain’s revenue growth involves analyzing market size and share, alongside direct factors like the number of gyms and membership fees.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) and Gross Margin<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Forecasting COGS requires understanding its relationship with revenue. The assumption of a stable gross margin can simplify forecasting, but attention must be paid to factors that may cause fluctuations, such as input costs or market share changes.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

SG&A Expenses Forecasting<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

While SG&A expenses<\/b> are less sensitive to sales volume changes, they encompass fixed and variable components. Predicting these expenses involves considering inflation, operational expansion, and sales-driven costs like commissions.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

Working Capital Considerations<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Key working capital components include inventory, receivables, and payables. Utilizing formulas based on days of inventory on hand (DOH), days sales outstanding (DSO), and number of days payable (DPO) aids in accurate forecasting.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
