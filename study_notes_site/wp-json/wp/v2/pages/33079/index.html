Capital Sources: Equity vs. Debt<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

Corporations can raise capital through:<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    \n
  • Ownership capital (equity)<\/b>: Funds provided by shareholders.<\/li>\n\n\n\n
  • Debt capital<\/b>: Funds provided by lenders, such as bank loans or bonds.<\/li>\n<\/ul>\n\n\n\n

    Debt obligations must be fulfilled, and lenders have priority in claim over shareholders in liquidation scenarios. Shareholders, on the other hand, have residual claim to the net assets of the company.<\/p>\n\n\n\n

    Risk & Return Profiles for Lenders & Shareholders<\/h2>\n\n\n\n

    Shareholders face higher risk due to share price volatility but have the potential for unlimited upside. Lenders, however, have more predictable cash flows and lower risk, but a capped upside based on the interest they receive. As a result, equity investors generally require higher returns than lenders.<\/p>\n\n\n\n
